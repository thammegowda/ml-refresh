[#sol-ex-decoding-filters.solution]
====
Top-k with stem:[k=2] keeps the first two tokens and renormalizes to stem:[(2/3, 1/3, 0, 0)]. Top-p with threshold 0.70 also keeps the first two, because stem:[0.50] is not enough and stem:[0.50 + 0.25 = 0.75] crosses the threshold. Min-p with stem:[\alpha=0.30] keeps tokens with probability at least stem:[0.15], so it keeps the first three and gives stem:[(0.50, 0.25, 0.15)/0.90]. The two-token filters are more peaked.
====

[#sol-ex-decoding-speculative-proof.solution]
====
For each token, stem:[p-q = (p-q)_+ - (q-p)_+]. Summing over tokens gives stem:[0 = \sum_x (p(x)-q(x))_+ - \sum_x (q(x)-p(x))_+], so the positive and negative mismatch masses are equal. Also stem:[\min(p,q) = q - (q-p)_+], hence stem:[1 - \sum_x \min(p,q) = \sum_x (q-p)_+ = \sum_x (p-q)_+]. Multiplying the residual distribution by this rejection probability leaves stem:[(p(x)-q(x))_+], which added to stem:[\min(p(x),q(x))] equals stem:[p(x)].
====

[#sol-ex-decoding-expected-acceptance.solution]
====
The draft accepts at least one token with probability stem:[0.8], at least two with probability stem:[0.8 \cdot 0.7 = 0.56], and all three with probability stem:[0.8 \cdot 0.7 \cdot 0.5 = 0.28]. Therefore stem:[\E[N\] = 0.8 + 0.56 + 0.28 = 1.64]. The test simulates 200,000 independent verification steps and asserts a mean within 0.01 of 1.64.
====
