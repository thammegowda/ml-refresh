[#sol-ex-grpo-advantages.solution]
====
For stem:[(1,0,0)], the mean is stem:[1/3]. The variance is
stem:[((2/3)^2 + (-1/3)^2 + (-1/3)^2)/3 = 2/9], so the standard deviation is
stem:[\sqrt{2}/3]. The advantages are therefore approximately
stem:[(1.414,-0.707,-0.707)].

[#sol-ex-grpo-k3.solution]
====
With samples from stem:[\pi_\theta], let
stem:[u(y)=\pi_0(y)/\pi_\theta(y)]. Then
stem:[\E_{\pi_\theta}[u\]=\sum_y \pi_0(y)=1]. Hence

[latexmath]
++++
\E_{\pi_\theta}[(u-1)-\log u]
= 0 + \E_{\pi_\theta}\Big[\log\frac{\pi_\theta(y)}{\pi_0(y)}\Big]
= \KL(\pi_\theta\Vert\pi_0).
++++

[#sol-ex-grpo-normalization.solution]
====
Sequence-level normalization gives the two completions equal weights stem:[(1/2,1/2)]. Token-level
normalization divides by the total of stem:[8] tokens, giving weights stem:[(2/8,6/8)].

With sequence-level weighting, a short answer and a long answer can have equal influence. With
token-level weighting, the long answer contributes more token gradients. This is why length policy
and loss normalization cannot be separated.
