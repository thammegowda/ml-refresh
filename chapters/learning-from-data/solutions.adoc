[#sol-ex-learning-from-data-risk.solution]
====
Population risk is stem:[\E[\ell(f_\vtheta(\vx), y)\]], the average loss on future examples from
the deployment distribution. Empirical risk replaces that expectation with a sample mean over
the training set. The estimate can be biased if the training set was collected from a different
population, filtered by a previous model, deduplicated incorrectly, or contaminated with test
examples. More examples reduce sampling noise but do not fix a mismatched sampling process.
====

[#sol-ex-learning-from-data-normal-equations.solution]
====
Start with stem:[L(\vtheta)=\frac1N(\mX\vtheta-\vy)^\T(\mX\vtheta-\vy)]. Expanding gives
stem:[\frac1N(\vtheta^\T\mX^\T\mX\vtheta - 2\vy^\T\mX\vtheta + \vy^\T\vy)]. The gradient is
stem:[\frac2N\mX^\T\mX\vtheta - \frac2N\mX^\T\vy]. Setting it to zero and multiplying by
stem:[N/2] gives stem:[\mX^\T\mX\vtheta = \mX^\T\vy]. If stem:[\mX^\T\mX] is invertible, solve
for stem:[\vtheta]; otherwise use least squares or regularization.
====

[#sol-ex-learning-from-data-logistic-gradient.solution]
====
For one example, stem:[p=\sigma(z)] and stem:[z=\vx^\T\vw+b]. The BCE derivatives are
stem:[\partial \ell/\partial p = -y/p + (1-y)/(1-p)] and
stem:[\partial p/\partial z = p(1-p)]. Multiplying gives
stem:[\partial \ell/\partial z = p-y]. The chain rule then gives
stem:[\partial \ell/\partial \vw = \vx(p-y)]. Averaging rows stacks those row vectors into
stem:[\mX^\T(\vp-\vy)/N], whose shape matches stem:[\vw] because stem:[\mX^\T] maps per-example
errors back to feature weights.
====

measures validation loss against the clean signal. Degree 1 underfits because it cannot bend.
Degree 11 overfits because it uses extra powers to chase noise. Degree 3 best matches the data
